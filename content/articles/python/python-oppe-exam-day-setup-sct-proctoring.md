Here is the quiet truth about the Python OPPE: plenty of students lose marks not because of their code, but because of their setup. They write good solutions, hit submit, and later find out the exam was never evaluated because a camera dropped off or the desk was not cleared. The proctoring rules are strict and a little unforgiving, so treat the setup as part of the exam, not an afterthought.

## Start with the SCT, not the day before

Before you get an OPPE slot, you have to pass the System Compatibility Test, the SCT. This is a dry run of the whole proctoring environment: your browser, your webcam, your mobile camera, and your internet. No approved SCT usually means no exam slot, so this is not optional and not something to leave for the last evening.

Do the SCT exactly the way you plan to sit the real exam. Same room, same table, same phone stand, same lighting. If something fails, you want to discover it now, while there is still time to borrow a phone or fix your connection. Read the current instructions on the [official BS Data Science site](https://study.iitm.ac.in/ds/) and in your term's OPPE notification, because the specifics do get tweaked.

## The dual-camera setup nobody explains well

The Python OPPE is a two-device exam. You need a computer with a working webcam and a mobile phone, and both cameras stay on the entire time. Students without both will not be allowed to sit the exam.

The mobile phone is the part people fumble. It goes on a stand, not propped against a mug, roughly two to three feet away and raised near your shoulder, angled so the proctor can see your face, both hands, the desk, and your screen in one frame. A stand is mandatory. Keep the phone joined to the Google Meet the entire time. If you drop off that Meet on your mobile, your exam can be treated as not evaluated, and no amount of good code will save it. Make sure the webcam on your computer is free too, not locked by some other app running in the background.

## What has to be on your desk, and what cannot

The rule is minimalist. On your desk: your computer, your mobile phone, blank white paper, and a pen. That is essentially it. Physical calculators are not allowed, and you should not have notes, books, or extra devices anywhere near you, including under the desk or stuck on the wall behind you.

Inside the exam, you work in the provided editor with a restricted set of tools, so no searching the web and no outside references. You get a rough sheet for logic, which is exactly why sketching before coding matters. If you want the allowed standard library methods burned into memory beforehand, the [Python documentation](https://docs.python.org/3/library/index.html) is the right place to revise, since you will be recalling all of it from memory on the day.

## The mistakes that make your exam "not evaluated"

These are the setup errors that actually cost people:

- Dropping off the Google Meet on the mobile at any point during the exam.
- The mobile camera stopping or getting knocked out of view.
- Forgetting to hit submit on the portal before time runs out.
- Not agreeing to the code of conduct in the portal.
- Turning up late. The portal typically opens around thirty minutes before and closes at the exact scheduled time with no extension, and latecomers are marked absent.

Have enough mobile data as a backup, since a fair chunk (several GB) can get used across an exam day. If you rely on a hotspot from a second phone, declare it to the proctor and keep it in view. Tell the people you live with not to walk in. A closed door and a quiet room are part of the requirement, not a nicety.

## A quick pre-exam checklist

Run through this the night before and again an hour before:

- SCT passed and slot confirmed.
- Phone stand positioned and tested, showing face, hands, desk, and screen.
- Both cameras working, webcam not used by another app.
- Desk cleared to only computer, phone, blank paper, and pen.
- Internet stable, backup data ready, room quiet and well lit.

With the logistics handled, your brain is free to do the one thing it should: solve problems. Keep sharpening the actual coding on the [Python practice hub](/app/subjects/python), and if you enjoy a bit of pressure, see where your solve times land on the [leaderboard](/leaderboard).

## FAQ

**Is the SCT the same as the OPPE?**
No. The SCT is a compatibility check you pass beforehand to unlock your exam slot. It verifies your devices, cameras, and connection.

**Do I really need two devices?**
Yes. A computer and a mobile phone, both with cameras on throughout. Without both, you will not be allowed to take the exam.

**What happens if my mobile camera or Meet drops mid-exam?**
That is a serious risk, since your exam can be marked as not evaluated. Keep the phone charged, on its stand, and joined to the Meet the whole time, and have a backup device ready.

**What is allowed on my desk?**
Just your computer, your phone, blank white paper, and a pen. No calculators, notes, books, or extra gadgets, and nothing hidden under the desk or on the walls.
